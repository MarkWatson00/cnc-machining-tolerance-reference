# cnc-machining-tolerance-reference
CNC machining tolerance quick reference by Thank You America LLC, Houston, Texas

A short reference for engineers and buyers who need to **specify tolerances on a CNC machined part**. Maintained by **Thank You America LLC**, Houston, Texas, which supplies [precision CNC machining services](https://tyallc.com/precision-machining/) (3, 4 and 5-axis milling, turning, wire EDM, parts up to 6 m, ISO 9001).

## 1. General tolerances (ISO 2768-1, linear dimensions, in mm)

Use these when a dimension has no tolerance written on the drawing.

| Nominal size (mm) | Fine (f) | Medium (m) | Coarse (c) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 to 3 | ±0.05 | ±0.1 | ±0.2 |
| over 3 to 6 | ±0.05 | ±0.1 | ±0.3 |
| over 6 to 30 | ±0.1 | ±0.2 | ±0.5 |
| over 30 to 120 | ±0.15 | ±0.3 | ±0.8 |
| over 120 to 400 | ±0.2 | ±0.5 | ±1.2 |
| over 400 to 1000 | ±0.3 | ±0.8 | ±2.0 |

On the drawing, write the class in the title block, for example: **ISO 2768-mK**.

## 2. Rules of thumb

- **Tighter tolerance means higher cost.** Tighten only the features that really need it (fits, bearing seats, sealing faces).
- **Tolerance the function, not the whole part.** Put close tolerances on critical features and leave the rest on the general class.
- **State the datum.** A tolerance without a clear datum is open to two readings.
- **Surface finish and tolerance go together.** A very tight size on a rough surface is hard to measure and hard to hold.
- **Large parts move.** Length, thin walls and heat change the size, so say which dimensions are critical.
- **Say how it will be inspected** (CMM, gauge, micrometer) for any critical tolerance.

## 3. What to send with an enquiry

1. Drawing (PDF or STEP) with tolerances and general class
2. Material and quantity
3. Surface finish and any coating
4. Critical dimensions and inspection requirement
